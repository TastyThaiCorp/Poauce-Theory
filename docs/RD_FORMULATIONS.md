## 1. Master Spherification SOP: Cold-Oil Agar Method

### Principle
A flavored liquid is thickened with agar (a plant-based gelling agent that sets when cooled), then dripped into a bath of thoroughly chilled neutral oil. The instant the warm droplet contacts cold oil, agar at its surface sets, forming a spherical bead with a thin gel skin. Beads are strained out, rinsed, and packed in a light syrup or brine.

Agar is the right choice for this product line: plant-based, halal, gelatin-free, and pork-free by nature. No calcium bath or chemical reagents are required.

### Base ratio (STARTING POINT TO VALIDATE)
**1.0 to 1.5% agar powder by weight of total liquid** (i.e., 10 to 15 g agar per 1,000 g liquid).

What the established practice supports vs. what Sauced must confirm:
- Standard molecular-gastronomy references use 0.5 to 1.0% for fluid gels and roughly 1.0 to 1.5% for firm cold-oil caviar. This is validated practice in restaurant kitchens.
- High-acid liquids (passionfruit, pickle brine) and high-sugar liquids (brown sugar syrup) change gel strength, so the effective ratio per flavor must be locked in by testing (see the agar concentration ladder in Section 6).
- Agar powder, not flakes: powder disperses and hydrates faster and gives more consistent results. Weigh it on a scale; do not volume-measure.

### Hydration procedure
1. Weigh the flavored liquid (minus any heat-sensitive additions) and the agar powder.
2. Disperse the agar powder into the room-temperature or cool liquid and whisk thoroughly before heat is applied. This prevents clumping. An immersion blender gives the most consistent dispersion.
3. Heat with constant stirring to a full boil, then hold at 85 to 95 C for 1 to 2 minutes. Full hydration requires this: agar dissolves at roughly 90 C and above.
4. Cool the liquid to a working temperature of 50 to 55 C before dripping. Do not drip at boiling temperature: droplets that are too hot will not gel fully before reaching the bottom of the oil column and will form deformed beads.
5. Add any heat-sensitive ingredients (volatile aromatics, fresh citrus juice, probiotic cultures) after cooling below 60 C.

### Oil bath setup
- Oil: neutral oil that stays liquid when chilled, such as canola or grapeseed. Avoid coconut oil (solidifies when chilled) and strongly flavored oils. Canola is the cost-effective pick at scale.
- Chill the oil in a tall, narrow container (a 2 L cambro or tall glass cylinder) to 2 to 5 C. Use a probe thermometer to confirm. Freezer chilling for 45 to 60 minutes typically gets there; do not let the oil freeze.
- Tall container matters: the droplet needs enough travel distance through cold oil to set before hitting the bottom. Target at least 15 to 20 cm of oil depth.
- Oil is reusable for multiple runs in the same session after straining; replace it when it clouds, smells off, or beads start emerging oily after rinsing. Track reuse cycles in the batch record (Section 3) and run an oil-reuse study before locking a reuse policy (Section 6).

### Drop technique
- Stage 1 tools: squeeze bottles, 60 mL syringes, or a caviar dropper.
- Hold the tip 3 to 5 cm above the oil surface. Steady, consistent drops give uniform bead size; taller drops produce smaller, more elongated beads and risk bursting. Calibrate height per flavor.
- Work in batches sized so the oil bath does not warm above 8 C. If beads start coming out misshapen mid-batch, rechill or swap the oil.
- If the liquid starts to set in the bottle mid-batch, rewarm gently to 50 to 55 C. Do not push partially gelled liquid: it makes lumpy beads.

### Strain and rinse
1. Let beads settle 2 to 3 minutes in the oil, then pour through a fine-mesh strainer.
2. Rinse thoroughly under cold running water or in two changes of cold water. Residual oil on the surface shortens shelf life and feels greasy in the finished drink.
3. Drain well, then pack into the final pack-off medium (light syrup or light brine, matched to the flavor family).

### Yield notes
- Expect roughly 0.9 to 1.0 L of finished beads per liter of flavored liquid, after hydration losses and rinse loss. Plan on 5 to 10% loss in the pilot stage. Measure and record actual yield per batch to tighten this.
- At roughly 0.25 mL per bead, one liter yields on the order of 3,500 to 4,000 beads. An 8 oz (227 g) jar at pilot scale is roughly 900 beads. This is an ESTIMATE; count a weighed sample to calibrate.

### Critical control points
| Checkpoint | Failure mode if missed |
|---|---|
| Agar fully hydrated (boil, hold 85-95 C, no dry specks) | Weak, fragile beads that break in the jar |
| Liquid at 50-55 C when dripping | Above: beads flatten on impact. Below: liquid sets in the bottle, lumpy beads |
| Oil at 2-5 C at start of run | Warm oil = flattened blobs, tails, no spheres |
| Beads fully rinsed of oil | Greasy mouthfeel, faster spoilage |
| Beads packed cold and refrigerated promptly | Food safety (see Section 5) |

## 2. Flavor Formulations: 1-Liter Pilot Batches

**Conventions for every recipe below:**
- Amounts are for 1 liter of flavored liquid BEFORE spherification; agar is additional at the Section 1 base ratio (10 to 15 g agar per liter, starting point to validate per flavor).
- Nutrition values are ESTIMATES computed from ingredient composition, per 50 g serving (finished beads packed in light medium, drained weight basis). Every value is labeled ESTIMATE pending database nutrition analysis and lab verification before final labels.
- Shelf life figures are WORKING ESTIMATES to validate by shelf-life testing (Section 6). Do not print them on packaging until tested.
- Halal watch-outs are flagged per recipe. Sauced's rule: no alcohol as preservative or carrier in any ingredient; verify supplier spec sheets.

### SWEET LINE

#### 2.1 Brown Sugar
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Dark brown sugar: 340 g
- Water: 660 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Molasses (unsulphured): 15 g
- Halal note: use certified-halal sugar. Some refined white sugar is processed with bone char; verify the supplier's processing aids. Dark brown sugar is generally lower-risk, but confirm the spec sheet. No vanilla extract in this recipe (alcohol carrier). If vanilla notes are wanted later, use alcohol-free vanilla flavoring only.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 120 kcal; 31 g carbs; 30 g sugar; 0 g protein; 0 g fat; 15 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light brown-sugar syrup. High sugar content helps; still refrigerate.

Use case: classic milk tea and brown-sugar boba drinks.

#### 2.2 Mango Passionfruit
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Mango puree (aseptic, no added sugar): 500 g
- Passionfruit puree: 250 g
- Water: 250 g
- Cane sugar: 120 g
- Agar powder: 14 g (starting point; high-acid liquid may need the upper end of the range)
- Citric acid: 2 g (pH target below 4.6, helps stability and flavor pop)
- Halal note: natural fruit flavors often use alcohol carriers. Specify alcohol-free or water-based flavorings in the supplier spec. Verify purees have no wine or alcohol-based preservatives.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 55 kcal; 14 g carbs; 12 g sugar; 0 g protein; 0 g fat; 5 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 14 to 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light passionfruit syrup. Acidity is an advantage here; confirm with testing.

Use case: fruit teas, lemonades, sparkling drinks, and cocktail garnishes (alcoholic or not).

### SAVORY LINE

#### 2.3 Spicy Sriracha
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Sriracha sauce: 500 g (verify brand; see halal note)
- Water or light vegetable stock: 480 g
- Rice vinegar (distilled, halal-certified brand): 20 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Halal note: many commercial sriracha brands list no alcohol, but verify the spec sheet for alcohol-based preservatives or natural flavors with alcohol carriers. Use distilled vinegar, not wine vinegar. If the chosen sriracha cannot be verified alcohol-free, switch brands before launch.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 45 kcal; 10 g carbs; 8 g sugar; 1 g protein; 0 g fat; 480 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 14 to 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light brine. Vinegar and salt help; testing still required.

Use case: ramen, pho, bloody marys, tacos, and spicy cocktail rims.

#### 2.4 Garlic Soy
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Soy sauce (certified-halal brand, verified alcohol-free): 400 g
- Water: 560 g
- Garlic powder (or roasted garlic puree, no wine): 25 g
- Toasted sesame oil: 10 g (flavor, not the spherification bath)
- Cane sugar: 15 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Halal note: THIS IS THE HIGHEST-RISK RECIPE FOR HALAL. Many soy sauces contain alcohol as a preservative (often listed as "alcohol" in ingredients). Source a certified-halal soy sauce or a verified alcohol-free tamari. Do not use mirin (it is alcoholic); if sweetness is needed, use sugar or mirin-style seasoning verified alcohol-free. ALLERGEN: soy is a major allergen; sesame is a watch-out (FDA recognized sesame as a major allergen). Both must be declared on the label.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 40 kcal; 7 g carbs; 5 g sugar; 2 g protein; 1 g fat; 900 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 14 to 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light soy brine.

Use case: dumplings, sushi and poke bowls, fried rice, noodle dishes.

### SALTY LINE

#### 2.5 Sea Salt Caramel
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Cane sugar: 300 g
- Water: 620 g
- Heavy cream (or coconut cream for dairy-free; decide one and stick with it): 80 g
- Fine sea salt: 12 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Halal note: use certified-halal sugar (bone-char processing watch-out). No vanilla extract (alcohol carrier); use alcohol-free vanilla flavoring only. If using dairy, verify the cream supplier has no alcohol-based additives.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 110 kcal; 27 g carbs; 26 g sugar; 0 g protein; 1 g fat; 180 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 14 to 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light caramel syrup. Dairy-containing version is the higher-risk variant; test it separately if both are made.

Use case: salted-caramel milk tea, iced lattes, dessert toppings, ice cream.

#### 2.6 Dill Pickle Brine
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Distilled white vinegar (halal-certified brand): 250 g
- Water: 600 g
- Cane sugar: 60 g
- Fine sea salt: 30 g
- Dill (dried dill weed): 8 g
- Garlic powder: 6 g
- Mustard seed (ground): 4 g
- Agar powder: 14 g (starting point; high-acid liquid)
- Halal note: use distilled vinegar, not wine or malt vinegar. Verify the vinegar is not derived from wine. Dill, garlic, and mustard seed are generally safe, but buy from suppliers that can provide halal documentation.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 25 kcal; 6 g carbs; 5 g sugar; 0 g protein; 0 g fat; 420 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light pickle brine. High acid and salt is the most shelf-stable profile of the eight; testing still required.

Use case: bloody marys, burgers, fried chicken sandwiches, pickle-back pairings.

### UMAMI LINE

#### 2.7 White Miso
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- White miso paste (certified-halal brand): 280 g
- Water: 660 g
- Cane sugar: 40 g
- Rice vinegar (distilled, halal-certified): 20 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Halal note: miso can contain alcohol as a preservative in some brands. Source certified-halal miso or a brand whose spec sheet confirms no alcohol. Add miso AFTER the agar hydration step, when the liquid has cooled below 60 C: boiling destroys miso's delicate flavor and aroma. ALLERGEN: miso is a soy product; soy must be declared on the label.

Nutrition per 50 g serving (ESTIMATE, pending lab analysis): 45 kcal; 7 g carbs; 5 g sugar; 2 g protein; 1 g fat; 620 mg sodium.

Shelf life (WORKING ESTIMATE, needs testing): 14 to 21 days refrigerated at 2 to 4 C, unopened, packed in light miso broth.

Use case: miso soup, ramen, dressings, roasted vegetables, marinade booster.

#### 2.8 Shiitake Soy
Ingredients (1 L liquid):
- Dried shiitake mushrooms, rehydrated and strained (use the soaking liquid): soaking liquid 700 g from 60 g dried mushrooms
- Soy sauce (certified-halal, verified alcohol-free): 180 g
- Water: 120 g
- Cane sugar: 20 g
- Agar powder: 12 g (starting point)
- Halal note: same soy-sauce risk as Garlic Soy. Source certified-halal or verified alcohol-free soy sauce only. No mirin, no cooking wine, no alcohol-based mushroom extracts. ALLERGEN: soy is a major allergen and must be declared on the label.

Use case: mushroom risotto, dashi-style broths, vegan umami boost for any savory dish.

## 3. Batch Standardization Checklist

### Pre-batch checks
- [ ] Recipe card pulled for the flavor; agar ratio confirmed for that flavor's locked value
- [ ] Agar lot number recorded; agar is within its best-by date
- [ ] All ingredients verified against the halal-approved supplier list for this flavor
- [ ] Oil bath chilling; target 2 to 5 C confirmed with a calibrated probe thermometer
- [ ] Thermometer calibrated (ice-water check: 0 C)
- [ ] Squeeze bottles, strainers, and pack-off jars sanitized
- [ ] Batch number assigned (format: YYYYMMDD-FLAVOR-##, e.g., 20261015-MISO-01)

### In-process checks
- [ ] Temperature log: record liquid temp at boil start, at end of 85-95 C hold, and at drop start (target 50-55 C). Log oil temp at run start and every 30 minutes.
- [ ] Bead size sample: every 30 minutes, measure 20 beads against a printed sizing guide or calipers. Target: uniform within 1 mm. Log the result.
- [ ] Taste check: every batch, taste 5 beads from the first, middle, and last of the run. Confirm flavor intensity matches the reference standard.
- [ ] Oil condition: note oil clarity and smell at each run; swap or rechill if beads deform.

### Post-batch checks
- [ ] Final yield weighed and recorded (kg of packed beads; jars filled)
- [ ] Beads fully rinsed (no oily sheen, no oil smell)
- [ ] Pack-off medium correct for the flavor family; jars sealed, labeled with batch number and pack date
- [ ] Product refrigerated at 2 to 4 C within 30 minutes of pack-off
- [ ] Retain sample: 1 jar per batch held for the shelf-life library, labeled with batch number

### Record-keeping template fields (every batch)
Batch number | Date | Flavor | Agar lot # | Agar % used | Boil hold temp/time | Drop temp | Oil start temp | Oil end temp | Oil reuse cycle # | Yield (kg) | Jars packed | Bead size sample result | Taste check pass/fail | Pack-off medium | Initials

Keep these records for at least 2 years. They are the backbone of the food-safety plan and of any halal audit.

## 4. Scale-Up Path

### Stage 1: Pilot (manual)
- Equipment: squeeze bottles, 60 mL syringes, tall cambros, fine strainers, probe thermometers, immersion blender, stock pots.
- Batch size: 2 to 5 L of liquid per batch.
- Equipment cost ESTIMATE: $300 to $800 total.
- Output: roughly 8 to 20 jars (8 oz) per batch. Suited to farmers markets, friends-and-family testing, and R&D.
- Trigger to move up: consistent weekly demand above what one person can produce in a day, or bead-size inconsistency above the 1 mm tolerance.

### Stage 2: Semi-automated (commissary)
- Equipment: multi-nozzle caviar dropper or peristaltic pump droplet system, jacketed kettle or heated holding tank with stirrer, blast chiller or glycol-chilled oil reservoir, larger strain and rinse station.
- Batch size: 20 to 50 L per batch.
- Equipment cost ESTIMATE: $5,000 to $25,000 depending on how much is bought new vs. used and whether the dropper is built or bought.
- Output: roughly 80 to 200 jars per batch. Suited to wholesale accounts and DTC fulfillment at real volume.
- Trigger to move up: order volume consistently exceeds commissary shift capacity, or a wholesale account requires volumes or food-safety documentation the commissary setup cannot support.

### Stage 3: Contract manufacturer / co-packer
- Find a co-packer with spherification or gelling experience, refrigerated capability, and willingness to run a halal-segregated line if halal certification is pursued.
- Equipment cost: none directly (their line), but expect minimum order quantities and setup fees. Budget ESTIMATE: $10,000 to $50,000 for first production run including setup, packaging, and initial inventory.
- Trigger to move up: volume exceeds what the commissary can produce, or unit economics at Stage 2 cannot hit the target margin at wholesale pricing.

## 5. Colorado Regulatory Path

Read this section as a checklist, not legal advice. Confirm every item with the relevant agency before selling.

1. **Cottage food is NOT an option.** Sauced beads are refrigerated and potentially hazardous. Colorado cottage food law covers only non-potentially-hazardous shelf-stable foods. Sauced requires a licensed commercial or commissary kitchen from day one.

2. **Licensed kitchen:** produce in a CDPHE-licensed commissary or commercial kitchen. Get the license in Sauced's name (separate company, separate license).

3. **CDPHE wholesale food manufacturer registration:** required to sell wholesale in Colorado. Application fee ESTIMATE: ~$100. Confirm the current fee and form with CDPHE.

4. **FDA facility registration:** required for interstate commerce (selling across state lines, including DTC shipping out of Colorado). Register the manufacturing facility with FDA.

5. **FDA nutrition labeling:** packaged retail product must carry a Nutrition Facts panel under 21 CFR 101.9. Process:
   - Step 1: database nutrition analysis from the recipes, ESTIMATE $50 to $150 per SKU.
   - Step 2: lab verification of the finished product for final labels, ESTIMATE varies by lab and panel; get quotes before committing.
   - Do not print final retail labels until lab values are in hand. The nutrition numbers in Section 2 are formulation estimates only.

6. **Denver retail food license:** if selling direct to consumers locally (markets, pop-ups, local retail), a Denver retail food license via the Denver Department of Public Health and Environment is required.

7. **Food liability / product liability insurance:** get quotes early; cost ESTIMATE $500 to $1,500 per year depending on coverage and revenue. Many wholesale accounts and markets require proof of insurance before they will stock the product.

8. **Halal certification (recommended given the brand promise):**
   - IFANCA: plant registration ESTIMATE $2,000 to $2,500, audit ESTIMATE $1,000 to $1,500, per-product fees ESTIMATE $350 to $750. Confirm current pricing directly with IFANCA.
   - Alternatives: ISNA Halal Certification and HFA (Halal Food Authority / regional bodies). Get quotes from at least two bodies.
   - Certification requires documented alcohol-free supply chain, segregated production, and the batch records in Section 3. Start the paperwork early; it takes months.

9. **Allergen labeling:** soy appears in Garlic Soy, White Miso, and Shiitake Soy and is a major allergen that must be declared. Sesame appears in Garlic Soy (sesame oil) and is a watch-out allergen. Dairy appears in Sea Salt Caramel if the heavy-cream version is used. Mustard appears in Dill Pickle Brine (not a US major allergen, but declare it voluntarily for transparency). Review every recipe against the current FDA major-allergen list before finalizing labels.

## 6. R&D Testing Plan: Experiments Before First Sale

Run these in order. Do not lock a recipe, print a label, or take a wholesale order until experiments 1 through 5 are complete for each flavor.

1. **Agar concentration ladder.** For each flavor, run 0.8%, 1.0%, 1.2%, 1.5%, and 1.8% agar batches. Score bead firmness, skin integrity, and mouthfeel blind. Lock the lowest concentration that gives structurally sound beads; less agar means better flavor release and lower cost.

2. **Bead size vs. drop height.** At the locked agar concentration, test drop heights of 2 cm, 4 cm, and 6 cm. Measure bead diameter and roundness (20-bead samples). Lock the height that gives the most uniform spheres for that flavor's viscosity.

3. **Shelf-life study at 7, 14, and 21 days.** Pack beads per the SOP, refrigerate at 2 to 4 C, and pull samples at each interval. Test: microbial counts (send to a lab), pH, visual/odor, texture, and taste. This study sets the real shelf life that goes on the package. The 14 to 21 day figures in Section 2 are working estimates only.

4. **Oil reuse cycles.** Track bead quality, oil clarity, and rinse effectiveness across 1, 3, 5, and 10 reuses of the same oil batch. Lock a maximum reuse count and a discard criterion (cloudiness, odor, or bead defect rate).

5. **Flavor intensity in the finished drink.** Drop finished beads into the actual end-use beverage (milk tea, lemonade, broth) and taste at 5, 15, and 30 minutes. Confirm the flavor reads through the drink and the beads do not over-soften. Adjust recipe concentration if the flavor washes out.

6. **Freeze-thaw test (only if frozen distribution is on the roadmap).** Freeze packed beads, thaw, and check for weeping, skin rupture, and texture loss. Agar gels can survive freezing better than gelatin, but confirm before committing to a frozen SKU.

7. **Pack-off medium comparison.** For each flavor, test beads packed in light syrup vs. light brine vs. the flavor's own liquid. Score flavor retention and bead firmness at day 14. Lock one medium per flavor.

**Definition of done for R&D:** locked agar % per flavor, locked drop height, validated shelf life per flavor, locked oil-reuse policy, locked pack-off medium, database nutrition analysis complete, and batch records flowing through the Section 3 template.
