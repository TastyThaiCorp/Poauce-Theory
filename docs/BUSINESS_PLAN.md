*Spherified sauced boba beads. Sweet, savory, salty, umami. Retail DTC + wholesale.*

## 2. The Opportunity

### Market sizing (researched estimates, cited by source)

| Segment | Size | Growth | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| US popping boba | ~$117M (2024) to ~$205M (2030) | ~9.9% CAGR | Grand View Research (estimate) |
| Global popping boba | ~$440-480M (2024/2025) | ~10-11% CAGR | Industry research estimates |
| US bubble tea market | ~$500M+ (2025) | ~7-9% CAGR | Industry research estimates |

Boba texture is mainstreaming fast in the US. Bubble tea has moved from niche to mall staple, and "popping boba" as a texture now shows up in desserts, cocktails, frozen yogurt, and coffee menus. The demand pool is growing ~10% a year, and the product format (spherified beads) is already familiar to consumers.

### The savory/umami gap

Competitive landscape (established players):

| Competitor | What they sell | Weakness vs Sauced |
|---|---|---|
| Bossen | Sweet fruit bursting boba; 7.04 lb tub wholesales ~$23.99, est. ~$3.20-3.40/lb via WebstaurantStore | Sweet-only; foodservice format, no retail brand, no savory line |
| Fanale | Sweet bursting boba, syrups | Sweet-only |
| Tea Zone | Sweet bursting boba, powders | Sweet-only |
| Monin | Fruit purees, popping boba | Sweet-only; premium brand, not a boba-first company |

**The gap:** Nearly every competitor sells sweet fruit flavors. Nobody owns the savory/salty/umami bead space. Nobody leads with halal certification positioning for this category. Nobody is building a DTC, creator-led brand for popping boba, it is all foodservice tubs.

### Why now

1. **TikTok food culture.** Texture-forward foods (boba, popping beads, cheese pulls) go viral reliably. A 10-second video of popping beads is inherently watchable, which is free distribution for a launch.
2. **Boba is mainstreaming.** When the format is this familiar, the barrier is flavor novelty, not education. Sauced only has to sell *new flavors*, not a new concept.
3. **Umami and global flavors are trending retail.** Miso, gochujang, pickle, and umami-forward products are having a DTC moment. Sauced rides that wave instead of fighting it.
4. **Halal is underserved in specialty food.** Halal certification is a real differentiator in a category where nobody is positioned for it, and it costs Sauced nothing extra since the formula is already plant-based and pork-free.

**Bottom line (estimate):** A small, growing category (~$117M US, ~10% CAGR) with a clear white space in savory/umami, low capital requirements to enter, and a free acquisition channel via TikTok.

## 4. Target Customers

### Segments

1. **Boba shops and bubble tea cafes (wholesale).** Independent shops buy cases of 12 at $6.00/jar ($72/case). They care about flavor variety, price per serving, and halal positioning for diverse customer bases. Bossen 7.04 lb tubs at ~$23.99 are the price anchor; Sauced positions above them on novelty and brand, not on price per pound.
2. **Restaurants and bars (wholesale, savory line).** Bars use Spicy Sriracha, Garlic Soy, and Dill Pickle Brine as cocktail garnishes. Restaurants use umami beads as chef garnish. Higher margin of attention, smaller volumes than boba shops.
3. **Home users (DTC).** Food experimenters, boba fans, gift buyers. Single jars $11.99, 3-packs $29.99, 8-flavor variety $79.99. The variety pack is the flagship DTC product: one purchase samples the whole line.
4. **TikTok food audience (acquisition channel).** Not a revenue segment on its own, but the engine that fills the DTC funnel at near-zero cost.

### Beachhead priority order

1. **Denver independent boba shops (wholesale).** Walking distance. Jor knows the local food scene. First 5-10 accounts prove product-market fit and generate cash.
2. **TikTok organic + DTC.** Launch content drives single-jar and variety-pack sales. Validates which flavors pop (literally and figuratively).
3. **Denver bars and restaurants (savory beads).** Chefs are easier to reach once retail social proof exists.
4. **Regional wholesale expansion.** Once Denver proves out, ship cases to independent shops in neighboring states.
5. **Distributors.** Only after production scale is proven. Distributor margins (typically 25-35%) compress the $6.00 wholesale price, so this comes last.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shelf life / food safety** | Medium | High | Spherified beads are a wet, perishable product. Mitigation: food-safety consulting during R&D; pH and water-activity testing; refrigerated shelf-life protocol; "keep refrigerated" labeling; product liability insurance from day one. Do not ship shelf-stable claims without lab validation. |
| **Copycats** | Medium | Medium | The process (cold-oil spherification) is public knowledge; the moat is brand, flavors, and speed. Mitigation: move fast, build the creator brand, lock wholesale relationships, trademark the Sauced name and flavor names. No patent play on the technique itself. |
| **Production scaling bottleneck** | Medium | High | Spherification is labor-intensive at pilot scale; a viral spike could outrun kitchen capacity. Mitigation: commissary with scalable hours; batch-size testing in R&D; cap DTC order velocity (pre-order/waitlist) rather than failing to fulfill; mechanized dripping equipment in year 2 if demand sustains. |
| **Flavor adoption risk (savory beads)** | Medium | Medium | Consumers may love the idea of Spicy Sriracha beads but not buy twice. Mitigation: the 8-flavor variety pack de-risks trial; track reorder rate per flavor; be willing to cut underperformers after 6 months and replace with winners. |
| **Regulatory** | Low-Medium | Medium | Food labeling (FDA), Colorado cottage/food manufacturing rules, allergen labeling (soy is a major allergen; miso/shiitake lines must label clearly). Mitigation: nutrition analysis + label review by a food-labeling consultant before print; allergen callouts on every label and the site. |
| **Oil supply / input cost swings** | Low | Low | Agar and oil prices can move. Mitigation: bulk purchasing once volume justifies it; unit cost has enough margin (~75% DTC) to absorb moderate input inflation. |
| **Founder bandwidth** | Medium | Medium | Jor runs Setpoint full-time plus Tasty Thai. Mitigation: Sauced is a nights-and-weekends pilot until revenue justifies more; batch production (one production day per week) keeps the time cost bounded. |

## 10. Key Decisions Jor Must Make

These are **his calls**, in rough priority order. Nothing downstream (labels, nutrition analysis, certification) locks until these are answered.

1. **Name lock-in.** Is "Sauced" the final brand name? Trademark search and domain availability check before spending on labels.
2. **Kitchen path.** Hourly commissary ($15-25/hr, pay as you go) vs membership ($350-600/mo, e.g., Kitchen Network Denver $100/mo + $20/hr or Spindle Kitchen $375/mo for 15 hrs + $22/hr overage). Hourly is cheaper under ~15 hrs/mo; membership wins once production is weekly.
3. **Final 8 flavors.** Are the 8 listed in Section 3 final? Nutrition analysis is priced per product, so every change costs money. Lock the lineup before ordering labels.
4. **Halal certification timing.** Launch with certified-halal ingredients and pursue formal IFANCA certification in year 1-2 (est. $2,500-6,000 first year), or certify before launch? Recommendation in this plan: launch first, certify from revenue.
5. **Packaging size.** 8 oz is the working spec. Consider a 16 oz foodservice jar for wholesale accounts later; do not split focus at launch.
6. **DTC vs wholesale emphasis.** The plan is built for both, but Jor's weekly hours are finite. Pick one primary channel for months 1-3 and let the other be opportunistic.
7. **Entity and accounting setup.** Form the LLC and open the separate bank account before the first dollar is spent. Sauced money never touches Tasty Thai accounts.
8. **Pricing confirmation.** $11.99 DTC single and $6.00 wholesale are working prices. Sanity-check against Bossen's ~$3.20-3.40/lb wholesale anchor and confirm the premium positioning holds with real shop owners during sampling.
